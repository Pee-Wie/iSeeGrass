About iSeeGrass

iSeeGrass is a hybrid web and mobile platform built for seagrass species identification and ecological assessment. It applies image recognition and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) classification to automate species identification from field photographs — reducing reliance on manual observation and improving consistency across surveys.

The species information library covers an estimated 60–72 seagrass species worldwide across 12 genera and 4 families, providing reference data for researchers and students. However, the image-based identification feature is focused on species documented in the Negros region — the geographic scope of the system's trained CNN model and dataset.

How to Get Started

  • Go to Identify and upload a clear field image of the seagrass.
  • The CNN model classifies the species and returns a confidence result.
  • Results are based on species found in the Negros region — the system's trained scope.
  • Save the result to My Observations for your records.
  • Review your activity summary on the Dashboard.
DID YOU KNOW Seagrass meadows cover only 0.1% of the ocean floor yet support around 20% of the world's biggest fisheries and sequester carbon up to 35× faster per hectare than tropical forests.

Key Seagrass Species — Negros Region (Identification scope · part of the broader ~60–72 species worldwide)

Enhalus acoroides

Tape Seagrass · Largest species in the world. Forms extensive intertidal meadows across Luzon, Visayas & Mindanao.

Thalassia hemprichii

Hemprich's Seagrass · Dominant species in Indo-Pacific mixed meadows; common across all Philippine regions.

Halophila ovalis

Dugong Grass · Pioneer coloniser with small oval leaf pairs. A primary food source for dugongs and green sea turtles.

Cymodocea rotundata

Rotund Cymodocea · Common on reef flats and shallow lagoons. Recognised by its rounded leaf tip.

Halodule uninervis

Spade Grass · Abundant pioneer species with a distinctive trident-shaped leaf tip. Key dugong food source.

Syringodium isoetifolium

Needle Seagrass · Unique cylindrical hollow leaves. Forms monospecific beds on sandy reef flats.
